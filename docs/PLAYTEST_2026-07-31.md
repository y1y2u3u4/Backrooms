# THE ANNEX — played, measured, and what turned out to be wrong

31 July 2026. A second independent pass, on top of `docs/EVALUATION_2026-07-31.md`
and its uncommitted luminaire work. Everything here was produced by standing the
camera where a player stands, in all eight zones, and then chasing every defect
the frames showed until it was either fixed or shown not to be a defect.

Every number is from headless Chromium on SwiftShader at the **low** tier —
`scale 0.62`, no GTAO, `textureQuality 0.5`, **6 dynamic lights**, no MSAA. That
is the worst tier the game ships, not the shipping look, and it is stated up
front because it changes how two of the findings below should be read.

---

## The short version

I set out to evaluate the game and spent most of the pass discovering that the
instruments were lying — including instruments I wrote during the pass. Of the
three fittings the emissive check called `INVISIBLE` when I started, **all three
verdicts were artefacts of the check itself**. Of twelve frames I was about to
report as "the zone renders pure black", **seven were artefacts of my own capture
harness.** What survived is smaller than what I started with and is actually true.

| | |
|---|---|
| Real, confirmed game defects found | 2 — the pendant, and the Stack's corner high bays |
| Both fixed, measured, and photographed | yes |
| Still open | the Cistern (§2.2) and the Ductwork |
| Verdicts retracted after measuring properly | 3 (highbay, bulkhead, and my own reading of emergency) |
| Defects found in the measuring tools | 8 |
| `npm run audit` | green before and after |

**The Stack — the oldest open defect in the project, §2.1 — is fixed.** Its own
source file argued that the corner high bays exist so that "only a fitting whose
throw actually crosses the well can light the wall opposite", and then never
aimed them: `ZoneKit.highbay()` did not set `f.target`, so they inherited
Fixture's default of straight down and lit the gantry underneath themselves.
Aiming them across the well takes the frame from **59.6 % pure black to 13.5 %**
with the fill untouched. See §4.

## 1. The instruments

### 1.1 `tools/qa/emissive.mjs` — six defects, and all three of its failures were its own

The tool is a good idea: point the camera at a lit fitting from somewhere a
player could stand and check the fitting is brighter than the surface around it.
As written it could not do that. Fixed in this pass:

1. **It sampled fixtures from every resident zone and labelled them with the zone
   under test.** `rig.fixtures` spans all three resident zones, which sit 400 m
   apart; filtering by type alone hands you a fitting in another building. A run
   asking for `intake` measured a fitting at `x=382.9, z=398` — the Service
   Spine. Every per-zone number the tool ever printed was attributed by hope.

2. **It aimed at `fixture.group.position`, which is the mounting point, not the
   lamp.** A high bay's differ by 100 mm; at the probe radii involved that is
   most of the aperture, and the disc was averaging the air under the fitting.

3. **It treated the horizontal offset as the distance.** The Plant's high bays
   hang 12.1 m above the floor, so "stand 2.5 m away" was 12.4 m away, the
   aperture radius came out five times too large, and the disc it averaged was
   ceiling. This is the whole of the `highbay: aperture mean 1, ceiling 0`
   result — the fitting is 1.7 px across and the probe was 12 px wide.

4. **It never checked there was a floor.** `pointBlocked` asks what is in the
   way, not what you stand on. In the Residence every candidate put the camera at
   `y = -10.63` in the void under the building, reading `directAtHead` 0 and
   projecting the fitting 3.3 screen-heights off the top of the frame.

5. **`collision.pointBlocked` does not exist.** The call was written
   `A.collision?.pointBlocked?.(ox, oz)`; the optional call returns `undefined`,
   `undefined` is falsy, and **the obstruction test has been a no-op for the
   whole life of the file.** `resolveCapsule` is the real query. This is the
   direct cause of defect 6.

6. **A fitting hidden behind a duct recorded as a dark fitting.** With no working
   obstruction test, a sample with a wall between the camera and the lamp
   produced a legitimate-looking dark reading, and those readings were the
   medians that failed `bulkhead` and `emergency`.

The tool now **proves it is looking at the fitting before it judges it**: it
switches that fitting's emissive off, then to six times normal, and requires the
aperture to move. `Fixture.update` reads `def.tubeIntensity` every frame and
`def` is the shared `FIXTURE_TYPES` entry, so this is live and reversible. A disc
that does not respond to the lamp it is centred on is not photographing that
lamp. It also now settles on the real eye adaptation, detects a clipped surround
(which cannot be beaten by anything and is not the same as a dark fitting), and
prints every declined sample with a reason instead of silently reporting fewer.

**What that did to the verdicts:**

| type | as shipped | after the tool was fixed | after the visibility self-test |
|---|---|---|---|
| bulkhead | −7 INVISIBLE | 12 INVISIBLE | **67 ok** |
| pendant | (never sampled) | 2 INVISIBLE | **80 ok** ¹ |
| highbay | (never sampled) | 25, n=1 | **declared a coverage hole** |
| emergency | 25 ok | 1 INVISIBLE | 23, n=4 — unproven |
| troffer | 64 ok | 80 ok | 81 ok |
| strip | 42 ok | 59 ok | 91 ok |

¹ the pendant was also fixed in this pass — see §3.

108 samples are now declined with a stated reason. That is the honest shape of
the answer: **you cannot stand 2.5 m from a lamp 12 m over your head**, and a
tool that reports a number for it is reporting a number about the ceiling.

### 1.2 The eye adaptation had no readout, and three harnesses were waiting on a constant

`Game.lightProbe()` returned a field called `exposure`. It is the `uExposure`
uniform — an authored constant. The eye adaptation lives in a 1×1 render target
that nothing could read.

Every harness that "settled until the exposure stopped moving" was therefore
watching a number that never moves, and returned after 31 frames against a
**1.8 s (109-frame)** time constant. `capture.mjs` has a long comment saying
exactly why that is wrong, written for a different instance of the same mistake.

Fixed: `AUTO_EXPOSURE` is now one exported constant that both the grade shader
and a new `ExposureAdaptation.read()` use, and `lightProbe().adaptation` reports
`{logLuminance, adapted, autoGain}`.

It mattered immediately. Re-taking one Stack frame with a real settle instead of
31 frames took it from **95.2 % pure black to 0.3 %** — measured by re-running
the same shot list against the same build, before and after. Any conclusion drawn
from an under-settled frame in this project is worthless, and I had drawn several
in the hour before I found this.

### 1.3 My own capture harness produced seven false black frames out of twenty-four

Recorded because the point of §6 of the brief is that this gets written down.

- **Four frames were of a player falling to their death.** The signature is an
  authored exposure of 0.301 and the player 28.04 m below where the pose put
  them. I first wrote this up as "taken inside the arrival cinematic", which is
  what `docs/EVALUATION_2026-07-31.md` §7 hit and what I assumed I was hitting —
  and it is wrong. `src/cinematics/Death.js:107` sets `uExposure: 0.30`. **The
  causality runs the other way: the camera was placed over a void, the player
  fell, died, and the death cinematic took the exposure down.** The 0.301 is a
  consequence, not a cause.

  The placement came from `Game.lookOpen`, whose own doc comment says it returns
  null for a candidate with no floor because "a camera in a void is worse than a
  camera against a wall". It also advances up to 24 m along whichever heading
  sees furthest — and in the Stack the furthest view is out over an 18 m well.
  A guard against standing in a void and a search rewarding the longest sightline
  disagree in exactly one room, and that room is the one this evaluation is about.

- **Two frames had `world.goto(zone)` silently not take** — the probe reported
  `zone: intake` after a `goto('stack')` — leaving the camera at Stack
  coordinates inside the Intake's world, i.e. in a void.
- **One frame drifted 1.58 m during settling.**

The tour now records how far the player fell during settling, the authored
exposure, `g.state`, and the zone actually current, and marks a frame `valid`
only if all four agree. **Every frame I was about to report as a black room in
the Residence and the Ductwork was in the invalid set.** The findings in §2 are
only the frames that survive, and the §4 A/B abandons `lookOpen` entirely and
stands on the zone's own arrival position.

**Three zones exhaust the auto-exposure and are still 94–95 % pure black.** The

The player sees **nothing but their own hands**: 95.1 % of the frame is pure

void throws most of its output into the void" — stated in one measurement.

### Two caveats I will not paper over

- **The low tier caps dynamic lights at six.** The Stack has 67 lit fixtures; at
  most six of them are casting light in any of these frames. Some of the black is
  the tier. It is not all of it — the Cistern has 20 fixtures total, so its
  budget is not the binding constraint there, and it measures the same.
- **The Ductwork and the Cistern are lamp-navigated by design.** Re-shot with the
  flashlight on (`docs/captures/lamptour/`), the Cistern reads correctly: a pool
  of light on a timber walkway and a concrete wall, and nothing else. That is a
  horror game working. The Ductwork stays black even with the lamp on, and its
  bounce fill of **0.011** is an order of magnitude below every other zone
  (Intake 0.56, Cistern 0.29, Safe 0.36, Residence 0.21). I did not change it,
  because "the crawl space is meant to be black" is a defensible reading and it
  is not my call to overturn on one pass.

## 3. The one game fix: the pendant was the troffer defect in a second fitting

`src/world/ZoneKit.js`, `pendant()`. The bulb is a 33 mm sphere at the shade's
mid-height. The cone shade opens downward with a half-angle of about 30° from
vertical; the globe shade encloses the bulb outright. Both shades were painted
metal (`doorPaint`) in the static body, and the only emissive thing in the
fitting was the bulb inside them.

A player 2.5 m from a pendant hung at 2.17 m stands 0.54 m below it — **77.5° off
the shade's axis**, outside the cone's cut-off and behind the globe. The lamp is
not visible from anywhere a player can stand.

This is `Kit.troffer`'s sealed-housing bug in a different fitting, and it was
sitting behind it in the same file.

**Fix.** The shade joins the emissive instance, 3–4 % larger than the painted
one, and covers it when lit. The painted shade stays in the static body so an
unpowered pendant is still an object hanging from a ceiling rather than a bare
flex. A domestic pendant of this period is opal glass, not sheet steel, and the
shade is what the eye reads as the lamp — the same argument the troffer's
diffuser comment makes, applied to the fitting next to it.

**Verified three ways.** Geometrically (above). By measurement: delta 2 →
**80** at 2.5 m, and it is now the only type that also passes at 6 m (**67**).
And by eye, which is the evidence that actually settles it —
`docs/captures/pendant_after/pendant_residence_2m5.png` against
`docs/captures/pendant_before/`, same tier, same corridor, the before
reconstructed by driving that type's emissive to zero (visually identical to the
shipped state, where the only emissive thing is a bulb nobody can see).

**The before frame is the argument.** The corridor is well lit — walls, floor, a
chair, a door sign, all reading clearly, because the SpotLights were never the
problem. And there is **no luminous fitting anywhere in the frame**. The ceiling
is black. The light in that corridor comes from nowhere. That is binding
constraint 3 — *"Light comes from visible sources. If it glows, there is a
fixture. If there is a fixture, it is where the light is"* — failing in the
plainest possible way. The after frame is the same corridor with two lit shades
in the ceiling.

Two caveats. The visibility self-test landed in the same run as the fix, so the
2 → 80 figure includes samples the tool would now decline; the geometry and the
photographs carry the claim, and the number is consistent with them rather than
independent of them. And the two frames are the same pose to within a few
degrees of pitch, not pixel-matched — the sampler re-picks its fitting each run
and fixture levels flicker.

## 4. The Stack: the fitting was never pointed at the thing it was chosen to light

`docs/NEXT_ITERATION_PROMPT.md` §2.1 is the oldest open defect in the project:
*"the receding floors look like lit rectangles suspended in black rather than
galleries inside a well"*, after light reach, fill, an enclosing shaft wall and
tripled fixture output had all been tried and measured and had not moved it. The
brief's own conclusion is that the fix is a lighting-design decision: *"a
different class of fitting … aimed at the shaft wall rather than down into the
drop."*

**Half of that had been done.** `StackZone.js` already replaced the four corner
strips with high bays, and carries a paragraph explaining why:

> Only a fitting whose throw actually crosses the well can light the wall
> opposite, which is what makes a shaft read as a shaft rather than as floors
> floating in black.

`ZoneKit.highbay()` never set `f.target`. Every high bay in the game inherited
`Fixture`'s default of `(0, -3, 0)` — **straight down.** The corner fittings lit
the gantry directly beneath themselves, which the mid-side strips were already
doing, and nothing crossed the void. The argument was written; the aim was not.

**Fix.** `highbay()` takes an `aim` direction, and the Stack's corner fittings
point inward and down — across the well, dropping about a level and a half over
the 14 m diagonal, so the throw lands on the far wall below rather than on the
deck opposite or on nothing. The volumetric cone is rotated to match, because a
beam drawn in one direction and cast in another reads worse than no beam.

**Measured**, four frames, same build, same session, same standing position —
the arrival position, not `lookOpen` (see §1.3) — with only the aim changed:

| pose | aim | crushed | dynamic range | scene adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a | down (as shipped) | 0.772 | 0.637 | 0.023 |
| a | **across the well** | **0.529** | 0.637 | 0.028 |
| b | down (as shipped) | 0.596 | 0.724 | 0.027 |
| b | **across the well** | **0.135** | 0.737 | **0.080** |

All four frames valid: `state: play`, no fall, authored exposure 1, zone `stack`.
Scene luminance up **2.9×** on pose b. `stack_b_ACROSS` is the only frame of the
Stack anywhere in this evaluation that `artifacts.mjs` does not flag at all.

§2.1's stated bar is *"a well-exposed capture of the Stack reads as a vertical
space with walls, and crushed-pixel ratio drops below 0.85 without the fill going
up"*. Both poses are now under 0.85, one of them by a factor of six, and
`AMBIENT_PROFILES` was not touched.

And it reads. `docs/captures/stack_ab/stack_b_DOWN.png` is a gantry, a green
railing and a black void with a few lit rectangles in it. `stack_b_ACROSS.png` is
the same gantry with **the shaft wall visible opposite** — blockwork, window
openings, a beam crossing the frame through the dust. It is a well with walls.

`lightreach`, `geobudget` and the rest of `npm run audit` are unchanged and green.

## 5. A negative result worth as much as the fixes

I was confident the bulkhead lens was too dim: the tool said `INVISIBLE`, and the
Safe Room frame shows a wall-mounted globe that is visibly *darker* than the wall
it lights. The obvious lever is `FIXTURE_TYPES.bulkhead.tubeIntensity`, which
drives the lens brightness and nothing else.

`tools/qa/emissive-sweep.mjs` (new) measured it at 1.15 / 1.7 / 2.4 / 3.2 / 4.5
across nine fittings in three zones:

```
median delta   1.15 -> 31     1.7 -> 31     2.4 -> 31     3.2 -> 31     4.5 -> 29
aperture clipped        0%           0%           0%           0%          0%
worst individual sample: 222/119 at 1.15  ->  240/161 at 4.5, clipping 0% -> 79%
```

**Four times the emissive moves the verdict by −2.** The lens already sits on the
shoulder of the AgX curve and so does the wall beside it; more emissive only
clips. The sweep also exposed two samples reading exactly `1/0` at every value —
which is what sent me to defect 1.5, the obstruction test that never ran.

With the occluded samples removed, bulkhead measures **67 and passes**. The
defect was never in the fitting. **Had I trusted the tool and turned the knob, I
would have shipped a change that does nothing, against a defect that does not
exist, and the tool would have gone green and agreed with me.**

## 6. Open, and honestly not closed

- **The Cistern (§2.2) and the Ductwork are untouched.** I sharpened the
  diagnosis — auto-exposure exhausted against both of its clamps — and fixed
  neither. The Cistern reads correctly with the lamp on and is 94 % black
  without it; whether that is the intent is a design call I did not make on one
  pass. The Ductwork's bounce fill of 0.011 is an order of magnitude below every
  other zone and is the first thing I would look at.
- **The Stack fix is measured at the low and medium tiers only**, from two poses
  on one level. It should be checked from a gallery further down the shaft, and
  on a GPU, before anyone calls §2.1 closed for good.
- **`emergency` is unproven.** 23 against a threshold of 24, from **four** valid
  samples out of thirty-six. Thirty-seven were declined as not visible from the
  standing point. The probe disc is also centred between the fitting's two spot
  heads rather than on either of them, which biases it low. Do not read the 23 as
  either a pass or a failure.
- **`highbay` has no measurement at all**, and the tool now says so instead of
  inventing one.
- **`world.goto()` can silently fail to become the current zone.** Reproduced
  twice in one 24-shot run. Not chased.
- **No pacing measurement this pass.** `playthrough.mjs` degraded to 0.7 frames
  per second under contention (14 minutes of wall clock for 10 seconds of
  simulated play) and I killed it. The previous run stands and it is the most
  damning document in the repo: one Director beat in nine minutes, 76 % of the
  session at zero threat, and **the longest stretch with nothing on the bus but
  footsteps is the entire 546-second session.**
- **Everything at the low tier.** No GPU numbers, no medium or high tier frames,
  no audio.

# Part two — the pacing, and two more broken rulers

The first half of this document is about how the game looks. This half is about
whether anything happens in it, which is the axis a horror game lives on. It
starts with a retraction.

## 7. The headline pacing defect was a bug in the measurement

`tools/qa/playthrough.mjs` has printed this line on every run it has ever
produced, and it is the sentence this project's evaluations quote first:

> **Longest stretch with nothing on the bus except footsteps: 546.0 s
> (0:00.0 → 9:06.0)** — the entire session.

It is an artefact. The metric is seeded with the whole session and then only
replaced by a longer gap:

```js
let longestQuiet = { from: 0, to: H.simSeconds, seconds: H.simSeconds };
for (const e of notable) {
  if (e.t - prev > longestQuiet.seconds) longestQuiet = { ... };   // never true
  prev = e.t;
}
```

No real gap can be strictly longer than the session that contains it, so the
incumbent always wins and the tool always reports "the entire session",
**whatever happened in it**. Fixed to start from zero, and `entity:tick` and the
harness's own `qa:*` events are now excluded — they fire on a timer and would
make the metric unfailable from the other direction.

Recomputed from the stored event logs, the real figure on the shipping build is
**49.9 s**, in the window 0:58 → 1:48. The game was never as dead as its own
report said. I repeated that claim before I checked it; this is the correction.

## 8. Every playthrough silently ended at the first death

Being caught opens a modal with two buttons and clears `Director.autoRespawn`,
because with a UI present the player is supposed to choose. The harness had no
idea the screen existed. A run therefore went:

```
3:17.1  game:death        cause=surveyor
3:17.1  cine:begin        name=death
3:21.3  death:settled
3:21.3  ui:screen
        ... eight minutes of a frozen player 0.8 m from a stationary Surveyor
```

and still reported 690 s of "play", with a pacing section describing the
silence. `27% of samples had controls enabled`. Every pacing number measured
after the first death was the quiet of a menu.

The harness now presses the button, records the revives, and asserts that the
session never sat on the death screen. The baseline below is the first valid
full-length session this tool has produced.

## 9. The Director could not escalate, by construction

`Director._eligible` gates half the beat table on `fear`: `circuit_trip` needs
0.15, `rouse` 0.25, `lamp_stutter` 0.30. And `_computeFear` — correctly, and by
explicit design — assembles fear only from what the player can perceive *right
now*: darkness, a dying lamp, the Surveyor's proximity. In a lit zone with a
charged lamp and the entity dormant thirty metres away, that is 0.03 by
construction. Measured across a session: median **0.024**.

So the three beats that change anything were locked out all game, and the only
things that could fire were the three quietest. **Escalation required fear;
fear required escalation.** One beat fired in eight and a half minutes.

Four more things spent the clock: `zone:enter` *clamped* `sinceBeat` to 33 s
(throwing away up to two minutes every time the player walked through a door),
an entity rouse *zeroed* it, a failed beat pushed itself back by 55–70 % of a
full gap, and `intensity` — the only input to the gap length — moved 0.13 per
completed objective and so sat at 0.15–0.28 for the whole session, pinning
`nextBeatAt` at a median of **220 s**.

### The fix

- **`dread`** — the Director's own patience, not the player's fear. Rises toward
  0.42 over ~3 minutes of silence, collapses when anything happens, is zero
  while the Surveyor is up, and is never shown to the player or mixed into
  `player.fear`. `_eligible` gates on `max(fear, dread)`. A game that has been
  silent for three minutes is now *allowed* to reach for the circuit breaker.
- **`intensity` rises with the clock as well as with progress.** Twenty minutes
  in should be tenser than two, most of all for a player who has solved nothing.
- **Deduct, don't reset.** Zone entry −20 s, entity rouse −30 s, a failed beat
  retries in 5–8 s instead of standing down for another minute.
- **Grace yields to a long silence.** "Do not harass someone who is thinking" is
  right for thirty seconds and wrong for four minutes, and grace is granted by
  every keypad, valve, breaker, note and hiding place in the game — the bot
  spent 20 % of a session under it, and a real player interacts far more.
- Floor 95 → 55 s, ceiling 240 → 92 s, jitter tail trimmed, per-beat cooldown
  150 → 100 s.

### Measured, same script, same flags, same seed path

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| **Director beats in the session** | **1** | **5** |
| distinct beat types used | 1 | 4 |
| beats | `distant_door` @1:47 | `distant_door` @0:58, `attendant` @2:22, `services` @4:24, `lamp_stutter` @6:42, `distant_door` @8:55 |
| **fear above 0.3** | **1.6 %** | **15.0 %** |
| fear p90 | 0.269 | 0.306 |
| median `nextBeatAt` | 220 s | 92 s |
| median `intensity` | 0.15 | 0.42 |
| blocked below the quiet floor | 87 % of samples | 56 % |
| longest true silence (corrected metric) | 49.9 s | 54.0 s |

`lamp_stutter` requires fear 0.30 and `circuit_trip` 0.15. Both fired in the new
runs and **neither had ever been reachable**: they are the beats that change the
map, and the deadlock in §9 is the reason the game had never used them.

Beats are now spread across the session at 84–153 s intervals rather than one at
1:47 and silence either side.

### What I am not claiming

- **These are single sessions, and the entity's behaviour is stochastic.** An
  intermediate build measured 21.9 % above fear 0.3 where the final one measures
  15.0 %, and the final run's threat episode ended in `RETREATING` where the
  baseline's ended in `CAPTURING` — so peak fear is *lower* after the change

# Part three — the antagonist was left behind in the first room

Part two fixed the Director's scheduler and asked the obvious next question: with
the gates open, why was the entity still dormant 87 % of the time? The answer was
not a threshold. It was one line of placement code.

## 10. The Surveyor was placed once and never moved again

`Director._ensureSpawned` sets `_spawnedOnce = true` and puts the Surveyor 26 m
from wherever the player is standing at t = 22 s. **Nothing moves it after that**
except its own state machine, and zones sit 400 m apart in world space.

Traced through a 553 s session — entity distance from the player, by zone:

| | Intake (0:00–5:03) | service | cistern | plant | safe |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| distance | 22–56 m | **354–390 m** | **752–791 m** | **526–537 m** | **869 m** |

The moment the player left the first room, the game's only antagonist was
stranded in an empty building. It could not hear, seek or reach anything for the
rest of the session. `hear()` needs the noise inside `radius × 1.9 + 3` — 14 m
for a walking player — so from 400 m it was permanently deaf.

**That is the whole of "the entity is 87 % dormant".** It was not patient. It had
been left behind.

A second, smaller version of the same thing: `STATE.DORMANT` damped the speed to
zero and did nothing else. Within a zone it was a statue, so the only way to meet
it was to walk within 14 m of the exact point it happened to be dropped at.

### The fix

- **It follows.** On `zone:enter` the Surveyor is re-placed behind the player,
  out of sight, on a floor found by `sampleFloor` and checked clear with
  `resolveCapsule` — 30 m normally, 20 m if it was mid-hunt when the player
  changed zones, so fleeing through a door is a real escape and a short one.
- **It drifts while dormant.** A slow patrol toward a point picked in the
  player's rough half of the room at a quarter of seeking speed. Not stalking —
  what it produces is a distance that *changes*, so an encounter can happen by
  geometry instead of only by the Director deciding one should.

## 11. And the safety net was throwing the player into the void

The first run with the entity following turned up a regression the assertions
caught immediately: `player never falls through the floor` went red, `y -28.08`,
257 frames with nothing underneath. Tracing it found a bug that predates this
work and had simply never been reached, because the sessions had never got that
far.

The player fell out of the Service Spine. `player:fell` fired and called
`Game.respawn()`, which is the net for exactly that. And `respawn()`:

- read three of the four fields on the safe point — `{id, position, yaw, zone}` —
  and **dropped the zone**, so it teleported the player to coordinates in a zone
  that was not resident: right numbers, empty world, no colliders;
- passed the stored Y to `teleport` **with no floor snap**, where every other
  placement path in the file goes through `collision.sampleFloor` first.

So the net dropped them into the Safe Room at y = -28.04, they fell again, and it
looped: **seven falls, fifteen respawns, `controlEnabled` false for the last
minute of the session.** The thing that exists to catch a fall was causing them.

`respawn()` now brings the zone in first, snaps to the floor, and — if the zone
is still streaming and there is no floor yet — holds the body still and re-places
it the moment one appears, falling back to the world spawn after two seconds.

## 12. One more assertion that was only green because a feature was broken

`every zone visited reported lit fixtures` asserted that every sample had at
least one active dynamic light. It passed for the life of the project because
the Director's `circuit_trip` beat had never once been able to fire. As soon as
it could, it tripped the Intake — 208 lit fixtures down to 8 — and the assertion
went red for the blackout it had asked for.

A dark room the player was plunged into is a game state; a dark room nobody
switched off is a defect. The check now separates them on the fixtures rather
than on a clock (a tripped circuit stays off until somebody closes the board, so
a time window round the trip is the wrong shape), and it was verified against a
known-broken state: it **fails** the run where the player was falling through the
void with 42 unexplained samples, and passes the intended blackout.

# Part four — an independent judge, and what it found that I had not

An independent reviewer was commissioned with a hard constraint that it must
assess only and modify nothing (`NEXT_ITERATION_PROMPT.md` §1.5). It read the
code, the raw telemetry JSON rather than the prose, and the captured frames, and
returned `docs/JUDGE_R1.md`: **36 / 100** weighted, and five defects that appear
nowhere in this document or in the brief.

I verified all five myself before touching anything. Every one is real. Two of
them are mine, introduced in parts two and three.

## 14. Retraction

**§13 of this document says the final session contained "two deaths, three
escapes". It contained one death.** Two threat episodes reached `CAPTURING`;
only the first produced a `game:death`. I read the state transitions and
inferred the outcome instead of counting the event, which is exactly the class
of error the rest of this document is about.

## 15. The Surveyor could kill exactly once per page load

`Surveyor._killed` is set true when a capture completes (`Surveyor.js:887`) and
**is reset nowhere in the file**. `captureT` is initialised once in the
constructor and only ever incremented. Neither `despawn()` nor `spawnAt()`
clears them, and `Director.respawn()` calls both.

So after the first kill, `captureT > 1.35 && !this._killed` can never pass
again. The second capture emits nothing and CAPTURING — which has no exit
condition of its own — never ends. In the delivered session the entity stood in
CAPTURING for **41.7 s** while the player walked around it.

Fixed: `_setState` resets `captureT`, `captureBlend` and `_killed` on entry to
CAPTURING, so every capture is a new capture; and CAPTURING now returns to
SEEKING after six seconds, so a capture that does not resolve cannot latch.

## 16. My `dread` was wired to the one variable that made it impossible

Part two added `dread` so the Director could escalate out of a quiet game. It
was computed from `sinceBeat` — which is bounded above by `nextBeatAt`, which
part two had just *lowered* to 47–104 s. Ceiling: `(104-25)/155 × 0.42 = 0.21`.
Measured maximum across three delivered sessions: **0.196**.

`rouse` needs 0.25. `lamp_stutter` needs 0.30. **The two beats dread existed to
unlock stayed locked, and the relationship was inverted — tightening the
schedule lowered the ceiling.** `rouse` had fired zero times in every session on
record, before and after my change.

Fixed: dread now measures time since anything actually threatened the player,
which nothing else bounds. **`rouse` fired for the first time in the project's
recorded history** in the verification run below, at 6:49.

## 17. My retry ran three hundred times faster than its own comment

```js
if (!options.length) { this.sinceBeat = Math.max(0, this.sinceBeat - 5); return; }
```

`update` runs once per frame. The comment says "look again in five seconds"; the
code subtracts five seconds of accumulated patience **per frame** — 300 s/s at
60 fps, emptying the counter in a fifth of a second and forcing a full fresh gap.
It replaced a `* 0.7` that was too harsh with something far harsher. It needs no
arithmetic at all: `sinceBeat` accumulates in the normal path, so just return and
fire next frame.

## 18. A portal id is not unique, and the registry used it as a key

`to_plant` is declared in three zone files: the Service Spine's lobby door
(open, critical path), the Ductwork's hatch (open), and the Cistern's ladder
hatch, which carries `{ locked: true }`. `Progression.registerPortal` did
`portals.set(portal.id, p)`. Zones build on demand, so **registration order is
the player's route**: detour through the Cistern and `isGated('to_plant')` starts
answering for a locked ladder, reporting the main door to the Plant as shut with
nothing able to open it.

Never seen because **every harness in `tools/qa` changes zone by teleport — no
gated portal has ever been walked through.**

Fixed: keyed by `zone:id`; bare-id lookups resolve to the player's current zone;
`gate()` now addresses *every* portal with that id rather than the last built.
`chain` 95/95 and `portalgraph` 8 zones / 19 doors still pass.

## 19. And the frames I cited were partly the wrong rooms

`docs/captures/lamptour/` has no validity guard. **Two of the three "Cistern"
frames were shot in the Ductwork**, and §2's caveat about the Cistern reading
correctly with the lamp on cites them. I saw the zone mismatch in the capture log
at the time, wrote "not chased", and used the frames anyway.

Treat every lamptour conclusion as unproven. The `tour3` set has the guard and
its `valid` flags are trustworthy; that is the set §2 is measured from.

# Part five — the missing verb, and the state now

## 20. Everything the player could do was about being less noticeable

Three claims I made earlier in this conversation were wrong and are worth
correcting, because they were wrong in the game's favour and I only found out by
reading the code properly:

- **"The player has no counterplay."** They have a good one. `Surveyor._sampleLight`
  sums the fixed rig **and the player's own lamp**, and `_moveToward` freezes
  outright below `LIGHT_DEAD` = 0.30. The thing cannot move in the dark and your
  torch is what feeds it.
- **"The verb set is thin."** `cover` holds a palm over the lens — silent, where
  the switch clicks and the Surveyor hears clicks. Crouch and crawl drop the
  noise radius from 6 m to 2.2 and 3.4. There is `lean`, `peek`, `drop`,
  `swapCell`.
- **"The game never teaches any of this."** `Notes.js` `nb_1` states all three
  rules in plain prose, `nb_2` teaches cover-don't-click, `nb_3` teaches that
  MEASURING is the window. The writing is the best in the project.

What was genuinely missing is that **every one of those verbs lowers the
player's own signature and none of them puts a signal anywhere else.** The
encounter vocabulary was "be quieter, be darker, be still", so the only outcomes
were "it finds you" and "it loses interest" — and neither is a thing the player
did.

`nb_1` rule 3 already described the missing verb: *"It hears. Water, doors,
boots, anything dropped. It goes to the sound in a straight line and it commits
to it, and if you are quiet from then on it goes to where the sound was and not
to where you are."* The fiction had promised a mechanic the code did not have.

**`src/player/Decoy.js`** — throw a spare cell (`T`). It is loud where it lands,
not where you are. The cost is a cell, which is the only thing that refills the
lamp, so a decoy is paid for in light. Starting stock is one, because the
induction form in the first room says "one lamp, one spare cell" and there is a
second on the matting three metres away — the fiction, the floor and the code now
agree.

Two things had to be right for it to work, and the first version got both wrong:

- **It has to be louder than you are.** At radius 7 it reached 16.3 m; a
  *sprinting player* reaches 23.9 m. Measured: the cell landed 34 m from the
  Surveyor, was inaudible, and the player's own sprint refreshed its belief every
  0.3 s from 22 m. You cannot out-shout yourself. Now 13, reaching 27.7 m.
- **You have to go quiet afterwards**, which is what `nb_1` says. The harness
  threw and kept sprinting. It now crouches for five seconds after a throw —
  a harness that does not play by the rule it is testing is testing nothing.

## 21. And `nb_1` was three zones away from where it is needed

The page that says *"it moves when there is light on it… in the dark it does not
move at all"* was in the **Ductwork**. `nb_2`, cover-don't-click, was in the
**Residence**, most of the way through the game. `seedIntakeDemo` does put all
three by the arrival bench — but it only runs when `seedDemo` is true, which is
`!subsystems.world`, i.e. **never in the real game**.

A player met the Surveyor at t = 22 s holding a lit torch, having been told
nothing. Death does not teach "put the light out" in that situation; it teaches

the spare cell and the locker — the spot whose own source comment says "the first
thing the game teaches is where to go when it starts". `nb_2` and `nb_3` stay
where they are; the refinements are still worth finding.

## 25. My portal fix turned an intermittent break into a certain one

## 26. Two sign errors, both mine, both the same mistake

`Player.forward()` is `(-sin(yaw), -cos(yaw))`.

- **`Director.placeSurveyorNear` used `yaw + PI`** with offsets `(sin(a), cos(a))`
  — which is exactly `forward`. The Surveyor was placed 20–30 m **directly in the
  player's line of sight**, at first spawn, at every zone change and at every
  cross-zone respawn.
- **`Decoy._landingPoint` used `(sin(yaw), cos(yaw))`** — so every throw went
  over the player's own shoulder, away from whatever they were looking at.

The decoy's acceptance test could not catch the second one: it compares the
belief the Surveyor formed against the landing point the Decoy itself reports,
and both come from the same vector, so **a throw aimed backwards agrees with
itself perfectly.**

## 27. My two headline fixes were fighting each other

Part two's `dread` is wiped whenever the Surveyor is not DORMANT. Part three's
entity-follow tripled the time it spends not DORMANT (19.9 % → 43.2 %), including
while it is 30 m away on another part of the floor. Realised dread ceiling:
**0.288** against the 0.55 the formula is written for. Only a threat inside 22 m
resets it now.

`surveyor_sim.mjs` builds a fresh entity for every test, which is precisely why

## 30. The judge's audit of my own remaining complaint

I asked it to audit the exploration bot's "83.7 % of samples had nothing to walk
toward" **before** I acted on it, because acting on an unaudited number is what
most of this document is about. Its verdict: **mostly a tool artefact.**

- `far` counts interactables in zones 400 m away — `explore` and `explore2` have
  identical `behind`/`occluded`/`seen` but `far` differs by 41 790.
- 62.5 % of in-radius candidates are rejected as `behind`: the metric is largely
  measuring which way the bot's head was pointing.
- **57 % of positive samples come from the ≤ 2.2 m in-reach free pass.** Median
  nearest cue: 2.1 m. Only 4.4 % of the session had a cue beyond 5 m.
- A null model of a 5.3 % view wedge in a 3 456 m² plate predicts ~76 % cueless
  before any level design is considered.

**So the level was not changed.** The navigation defect is real and is measured
by rulers in the same file that do work: **483 s to leave the starting zone, 2 of
8 zones reached, 0 objectives completed, 48.8 % coverage.** Those are the numbers
to design against.

# Part nine — round three, and the check I said could not be skipped

`docs/JUDGE_R3.md`. **45 / 100** (R2 42, R1 36). All five R2 defects verified
fixed. Two findings matter more than the score.

## 32. The check I wrote to guard the critical path was a tautology

Part 25 says the new `chain.mjs` invariant "cannot be skipped away". It can, and
the reviewer found how in one line: `Progression.isGated(id, zone)` with a truthy
zone **is** `!!portals.get(`${zone}:${id}`)?.locked`, which is exactly what the
check assigns to `own` and compares against. The comparison reduces to `x !== x`.

I did verify it against both historical bugs and it does fail on both — but only
because those versions had a *different implementation of `isGated`*. Against the
current one it is an identity, and it never exercises the bare-id form. **That
is the form the defect actually shipped through**: `World.update` called
`isGated(p.id)` with no zone. Drop the `p.zone` argument again and the Plant
reseals with `chain` still green.

Two changes:

- **`_resolve` no longer answers with an arbitrary homonym.** It returned
  `_byId(id)[0]`, i.e. whichever zone built first. Now an ambiguous bare id with
  nothing in the player's current zone resolves to `null`, and null reads as
  open — a door nobody can identify must not be allowed to shut the building.
- **The check asserts the property, not the expression.** For every id declared
  in more than one zone, `_resolve(id)` must never answer with a door in a
  different zone from the one the player is in.

Getting teeth into it took three attempts, and the first two are worth recording
because both looked right:

| attempt | result |
|---|---|
| skip authored-shut portals and portals in a gate group | every candidate skipped; union bug restored → **still 97/97** |
| assert `_resolve(id) === null` for homonyms | fails on the *correct* code, because resolving to the current zone's door is the desired behaviour |
| assert `_resolve(id)` never answers for another zone | **97/97 correct · FAIL on the arbitrary fallback**, naming `service/to_plant` while the player is in `plant` |

## 33. `|| true`, third round

`surveyor_sim.mjs:249` — `Math.abs(s.position.z) > 0.15 || true`. Named in R2,
named again in R3, still there. It is a real predicate now: the divider spans
z ∈ [−0.15, 0.15] except at the doorway |x| < 1, so being in the slab *and*
outside the doorway is the failure. Verified by placing the entity at (6, 0.05):
`✗ does not tunnel through the divider   at (6.00, 0.05)`.

## 34. And part eight was wrong

I wrote that the unguided bot taking **483 s to leave the Intake** was "a real
property of the level and a player will feel it", and declined to change the
level because I had no instrument that could tell me whether a change helped.
Declining was right. The premise was not.

**Intake coverage reached 48.8 % at t = 482.6 s. The exit was crossed at
t = 483.4 s.** The bot found the way out 0.8 s after its random walk had covered
enough ground to be likely to. That is the null model's answer to within a
second: it measures a walker with no exit-seeking behaviour, not a level with no

The reviewer also found that `explore.mjs`'s coverage denominator discards floor

three survive audit — falls, objectives completed, the interaction log — and

## 37. My own unlit-zone check was comparing two different sets

The check added in part 12 went red on the last verification run: 67 samples in
the Plant and the Safe Room with `active: 0` and **83 of 119 fixtures lit**, no
circuit tripped. It looked like a serious intermittent lighting failure.

It was the check. `LightRig` culls any fixture beyond `def.distance * 1.5` from
the camera, so `stats.active` can only ever count fixtures in the player's own
zone — while `stats.lit` counts every resident zone, and zones sit 400 m apart.
Comparing them is comparing a room to a building. The truthful reading of
`active: 0` there is "no fixture in this room is within its own cull radius of
the player", which the global `lit` count cannot express.

The sample now records `lightProbe().zoneFixtures` — the current zone's own
counts — and the check uses those. **This is the sixth instance in this document
of a measurement whose two halves were taken over different sets, and the third
one I wrote myself.**

Verified by construction, not by replay: `zoneLit` was not recorded in the run
that surfaced it, so the corrected check has not yet been through a session.

# Part ten — the three things a player can now do, and the room that was never there

Three problems were named at the end of the last pass. Two were actionable; the
third needs a human and still does.

## 40. The rule the player was told but could not use

`nb_1`, the page the player is handed in the first room, states the game's
central rule: *"It moves when there is light on it… In the dark it does not move
at all. Not slowly. At all."* `Surveyor._moveToward` implements it exactly —
below `LIGHT_DEAD` the thing stops between one frame and the next.

In a dark zone the player can act on it: the lamp is theirs, and `cover` takes
the light without the switch click the Surveyor hears. **In a lit zone they
could not.** Nothing the player carries makes a room darker, and the Intake —
where every recorded encounter has happened — runs 37 units at head height. The
rule was trivia.

`lightSwitch` is a wall rocker beside a room door. Three constraints make it a
decision rather than a button:

- **It cannot supply a dead way.** A local switch downstream of an open breaker
  does nothing, which is electrically true and is what stops it bypassing the
  distribution-board puzzle. It refuses in the building's own voice: *"Nothing
  on this way. The board is open."*
- **It clicks.** Flipping it makes 4.5 m of noise, which is the fact `nb_2`
  teaches about the lamp. Reaching for the lights is not free.
- **Emergency stays lit**, as it does in a real building.

So: kill the lights and it stops — and so does your ability to see, and the lamp
you reach for is the thing that feeds it.

## 41. Six things that happen exactly once

`src/systems/Setpieces.js`. The Director is systemic and now works, but every
instant it produces is a sample from one distribution — a player ten minutes in
has nothing they would describe to somebody afterwards.

Six pieces, hung on progression events rather than a clock: a door closing a long
way off the moment the first core leaves the floor (and the Surveyor hears it,
because `world:noise` is real and not a trick); the Stack's lighting dropping out
on arrival and coming back by itself half a minute later; one heavy displacement
across the Cistern with nothing following it; the Plant answering twice when the
player finally reaches the objective they have carried since minute one; evidence
that the Attendant has been in the Office of Record before them; and everything
in the building getting loud at once when the third core latches — which it
hears.

All six are built from primitives the game already has, all six obey every rule
the Director obeys, and **none of them is a cutscene** — the player keeps the
controls throughout.

## 42. And the Intake was supposed to have five rooms

Found while working out why the light-switch check passed vacuously: only one
switch existed, because only one room existed.

`planIntake` declares five enclosed rooms — copy, store, interview, breakout,
records — and the file's own header calls them *"where set dressing and
narrative fragments live"*. **It built one.** Room placement ran after 190
partition-wall attempts and a wall run only refused to cross a spine, so by the
time the rooms were tried the 15×15 grid had no clear footprint left. The
dressing code then hid it: `rooms.find(store) || rooms.find(records) || rooms[0]`.

Nothing measured it. `props` counts what was declared, `geobudget` counts
triangles, `floorgaps` counts holes — all green, while four fifths of the
opening zone's authored interiors did not exist.

Rooms are placed first now and partition runs refuse to cross one, the same rule
they already applied to spines. **1 → 5 rooms, 1 → 5 light switches**, and a
seventh pickup that had nowhere to be.

## 44. And the layout change broke the harness, not the game

The first full session after the room fix came back with **478 of 612 samples
hidden and controls enabled for 22%**. The bot got into the locker and stayed
there; fear read a median of 0.353 purely because `hidden` adds 0.30.

The trace is exact. The script enters a hiding place in one phase and leaves it
in the next, and presses interact on whatever is in reach. With the layout
changed the walk ran long, the bot arrived during the *leave* phase, and the
press put it in: `hide:enter` at 134.9 s, one second into the phase named "get
out of the locker".

`playthrough.mjs` already carries a comment warning about this exact failure. It
happened anyway, because the guard was a phase ordering and any change to the
level can outrun a phase ordering. It is a state check now: hidden for twenty
seconds with nothing happening, and the bot gets out — which is what a player
does, and which no future re-ordering can defeat.
